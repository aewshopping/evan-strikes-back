Each chapter should be short, average length 200 to 300 words. 

Prioritise character speech over descriptions. This is really important! Speech should be used to move the plot forward.

Use the Kurt Vonnegut rule that every sentence should either build character or move the story forward.

Each chapter should be short and exciting, and as many as possible should have a decision that feels real and interesting.

Any descriptions that are used should be short and clear. No very long passages

Language should be jargon free and simple. I am writing this book for Evan who will be 7 years old when he gets it, although it should also be suitable for an older child (it may be read to him).

Some star wars related technobabble is ok provided it is easy to pronounce! This is because Evan is a star wars fan and so I need some world building. 

Use Katherine Rundell's rule that we should have as many different types of love as possible, noting that in this story not much may be possible! B9-QT1's love for Evan and visa versa. Yodelihoo's love for her father Yodahoo. Evan's dad's love for him. Arthur and Evan love each other as brothers.

The writing feel should be light but with a hidden under current of darkness or humour that adults may detect. Sort of panto style in this respect, writing for two audiences.
